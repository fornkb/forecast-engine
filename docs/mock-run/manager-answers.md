# Haveli Harvest Oils: the planning manager's answers

You play the **planning manager** of Haveli Harvest Oils (a fictional company) on the web site.
The AI never sees this file. It only sees what you paste or type on the site.

Rules for a fair test:
- **Answer from this sheet and the [company brief](company-brief.md).** If the AI asks
  something not covered, say "Not known". Don't invent.
- **Never tell the AI about the future** (anything after October 2026). Finding that is the
  AI's job.

## 1. "About the company" (paste into *Draft a profile with AI*)

```text
Haveli Harvest Oils is a family-owned edible-oil company founded in 1998, with a mustard
expeller, a refinery and a packing plant in Bharatpur, Rajasthan, and a second packing unit in
Ghaziabad (Delhi NCR). We sell about 5 million litres a year under the HH brands: kachi ghani
mustard oil, refined soybean oil, refined palmolein, sunflower oil, rice bran oil and
vanaspati. Our markets are Rajasthan, Delhi NCR, Haryana and Uttar Pradesh, through three
distributors (Jaipur, New Delhi, Lucknow), one modern-trade supermarket chain, and a desk that
sells 15 kg tins to sweet shops, caterers and namkeen makers.

Mustard seed is domestic and bought at harvest (March to May). Soybean, palm and sunflower oil
are made from imported crude oil (Argentina/Brazil, Indonesia/Malaysia, and Russia/Ukraine via
the Black Sea), landed at Kandla and booked 6 to 10 weeks ahead. About 59% of our volume
depends on imported crude oil.

Demand peaks before Diwali, Holi and Dussehra and in the wedding season. Mustard oil sells most
in winter. Shelf prices follow raw-material costs within a few weeks, and shoppers and
caterers switch between oils when prices move. We need the forecast to plan crude-oil bookings,
mustard seed buying and price-list changes.
```

## 3. Paste-ready answers (into *Questions from the AI → Your answers*)

```text
Item groups: MUST = kachi ghani mustard oil, SOYA = refined soybean oil, PALM = refined
palmolein, SUNF = refined sunflower oil, RICE = rice bran oil, VANA = vanaspati. Please group
them as: mustard oil (MUST); soybean and sunflower oil (SOYA, SUNF); palmolein and vanaspati
(PALM, VANA); rice bran oil (RICE).

Raw materials: mustard seed from Rajasthan, Haryana and Madhya Pradesh (own expeller, bought at
the March-May harvest). Crude soybean oil from Argentina and Brazil (~70%) and Madhya Pradesh.
Crude palm oil and palm stearin from Indonesia and Malaysia. Crude sunflower oil from Russia
and Ukraine via the Black Sea, Red Sea and Suez. Crude rice bran oil from Punjab and Haryana
mills. Imports land at Kandla and are booked 6-8 weeks ahead (sunflower 8-10).

Region: Rajasthan, Delhi NCR, Haryana and Uttar Pradesh; main city New Delhi (28.61 N,
77.21 E), India.

Customers: distributors in New Delhi (31% of volume), Jaipur (25%) and Lucknow (24%); a desk
selling 15 kg tins to sweet shops and caterers (11%); one modern-trade chain (10%). No exports.

Seasonality: mustard oil peaks November-February. All oils peak in the three weeks before
Diwali and before Holi, smaller before Dussehra. Caterer tins follow the wedding season.
June-August is slow.

Prices: shelf prices follow raw-material costs within 2-4 weeks. Dearer imported oils sell less
and push some buyers to mustard oil; caterers switch between palmolein, soybean and vanaspati
on price; distributors stock up before announced price rises.

Past events that clearly moved sales: the January 2024 cold wave lifted mustard oil in
2024-01. The import duty hike of 14 Sep 2024 cut imported-oil sales in 2024-10, 2024-11,
2024-12 and 2025-01 (after distributors bought ahead in September 2024), with some switching to
mustard. The duty cut of 31 May 2025 brought imported oils back from June 2025. The May-June
2024 heat softened caterer tins.

The forecast already includes normal seasonality and festival months. It does not know future
duty changes, international oil prices, crop sizes, shipping problems or unusual weather.
Anything after October 2026: not known.
```

## 4. If the draft needs a nudge

After applying the answers, open *Company profile*:
- **Every driver should show "OK".** If one says "missing params", add the parameter in *Edit
  as YAML* (for example `region: north_india` for a weather driver).
- **Measured evidence.** If no driver has `analog_periods`, the AI didn't pick up the past
  events. You can add them by hand: on the import-duty driver,
  `analog_periods: ["2024-10", "2024-11", "2024-12", "2025-01"]`; on a winter-weather driver,
  `analog_periods: ["2024-01"]`. Python then measures those effects in the sales history
  (about −8% for soybean and sunflower after the duty hike, and +4% for mustard in January
  2024).
- **Compare with the answer key.** [`reference_profile.yaml`](reference_profile.yaml) is what
  an expert would set up. Note what the AI found, missed or added. That's part of the result.
